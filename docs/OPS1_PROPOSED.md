# CORTAC OPS 1 proposed operating protocol

**Proposal version OPS1-PROPOSED-1, 9 October 2026. Proposed only. Not ratified, activated, or an authority to act.**

OPS 1 specifies how an adopting institution would carry out six operating duties: resolve disputed facts, verify reviewer independence, consult before consequential policy changes, assess outcomes independently, give consistent reasons, and follow remedies through to their effects. The first implementation priorities are sections 1, 4, and 6. These procedures supplement [OPR 1](OPR1_PROPOSED.md); they do not replace its protected inquiry, equal consideration, cumulative-burden, authority, review, or resource safeguards.

OPR 1 already proposes evidence and counterevidence duties in section 4, verified independence and reassessment in sections 7.5–7.6, an opportunity to respond in section 8, version-specific remedies in section 12, and outcome reporting in section 13.5. OPS 1 makes their operational responsibilities and evidence requirements more explicit. Consultation and comparable-case procedures add detail without implying that existing principles were absent or already operational.

The repository's software and frozen design-profile versions remain as described in [CURRENT_CONTRACT](CURRENT_CONTRACT.md). The proposed offline `ops_trace_v1` and `ops_study_v1` extensions inspect supplied trace and study-plan relationships only. Their implementation status and remaining gaps belong in [OPS1_REQUIREMENTS](OPS1_REQUIREMENTS.md). Neither a complete record nor a passing test establishes factual truth, genuine independence, effective remedy, empirical benefit, ratification, or permission for external execution.

## 0 Common operating rules

**0.1 Existing roles and powers.** Assign these tasks to the existing epistemic assessment, normative assessment, decision, authorization and execution, and audit and correction functions identified in OPR 1 section 7. A task owner is an accountable assignment within an existing authorized role, not a new office. Preserve existing staffing, quorum, independence, registrar, and external-principal requirements. If no authorized independent assignment is available, record the gap and use an already authorized replacement or referral route; this protocol creates none.

**0.2 Exact context.** Each consequential record identifies its case or policy proposal, current version, applicable charter and policy versions, responsible role, supplied evidence versions, relevant time, and dependencies. Corrections preserve earlier records and identify what they supersede. An earlier review cannot silently resolve a later allegation, objection, appeal, independence change, or remedy version. A digest binds supplied bytes; it does not authenticate their origin or completeness.

**0.3 Proportionate process.** Use a short record when the issues are simple and uncontested. Expand investigation and hearing to match material dispute, risk, cumulative burden, and irreversibility. Reducing administrative formality cannot remove a required safeguard. Publish finite capacity, deadlines, retention, access, and escalation arrangements before operational use. Missing capacity remains an incomplete outcome, never a finding against an affected person or a claim of completed work.

**0.4 Decision and execution remain separate.** None of the following investigative findings, consultation responses, comparisons, assessments, or remedy reports supplies a grant, replaces an effect-boundary check, renews a restriction, or overcomes a protected-limit failure. OPR 1's rules on temporary containment, expiry, restoration, cumulative burden, and adoption continue to apply.

## 1 Resolve disputed facts before relying on them

**1.1 Define the issue.** The epistemic assessment owner lists each material factual proposition, the decision that depends on it, the applicable burden and threshold, supporting material, known contrary material, uncertainty, and the strongest material objection. Keep observations, source assertions, inferences, predictions, normative judgments, and authority findings distinct. The office seeking restriction retains the burden of justification.

**1.2 Inspect originals where available.** Obtain and inspect available original records and their necessary surrounding context within existing access authority. Record creator or source, acquisition route and date, version, relevant transformations, and who performed the check. Preserve an access-controlled original or faithful snapshot where permitted. Identify quotations, screenshots, summaries, translations, redactions, and model outputs as derivatives. Record missing originals and attempted verification; do not represent a derivative's digest as the identity of an unavailable original.

**1.3 Verify provenance and dependence.** Check material authenticity and provenance claims against available independent records, rather than accepting a supplied reference or self-description. Explain the verification method, findings, limits, and unresolved source dependencies. Copied reports, shared witnesses, common upstream material, and repeated model summaries do not become independent corroboration by multiplication. An unverifiable source may still be considered under the applicable standard, but its uncertainty must remain visible and cannot satisfy a necessary authenticity condition by declaration.

**1.4 Seek and test counterevidence.** Conduct a bounded, documented search directed at plausible alternative explanations and material exculpatory or contrary evidence. Record the scope, sources checked, relevant absences, and limitations. Put the strongest objection against the proposed finding in a form its proponent can recognize. Explain what evidence would change the finding. Merely attaching an objection or listing contrary material is insufficient if the decision does not address its force.

**1.5 Resolve material contradictions.** Link each material objection and contradiction to the exact claim and evidence versions it concerns. Give a reasoned resolution: corrected claim, withdrawn reliance, supported reconciliation, or unresolved issue with an account of its consequence. Do not mark a contradiction resolved because a response exists. If an unresolved contradiction defeats a necessary proposition at the applicable threshold, no final adverse finding may rely on that proposition. Any narrower or temporary action requires its own sufficient evidence and existing authority; uncertainty neither establishes guilt nor automatically supplies an access grant.

**1.6 Hear the affected account.** Before a final consequential finding, provide the evidence or a useful protected summary and a practical opportunity to correct facts, present alternatives, and challenge provenance or conflicts. Provide an oral or otherwise interactive hearing when a material credibility dispute, conflicting accounts, difficulty using written process, or the seriousness of the consequences makes it necessary for a fair determination. Record the hearing scope, independent responsible reviewer, accommodations, questions, responses, and effect on the findings. A reasoned decision that a hearing is unnecessary must identify the effective alternative. Do not require direct contact with an alleged harasser or treat nonparticipation, inability to respond, or silence as an admission. Bounded urgent containment remains governed by OPR 1 section 9, with prompt subsequent response and review.

**1.7 Record the actual reasoning.** The finding states which propositions are supported under the published standard, which remain uncertain, which objections changed the result, and why others did not. The decision function separately determines the normative and authority consequences. Preserve dissent and the challenge route. New material evidence or a materially revised claim requires reconsideration of affected findings, not reuse of an obsolete resolution.

## 2 Verify practical reviewer independence

**2.1 Identify the real dependencies.** Before relying on required independent review, its accountable verification owner checks actual arrangements for funding and resource withholding; appointment, tenure and removal; access to records, credentials and infrastructure; agenda or publication control; recusal and replacement; and access to independent advice. Identify material controllers and dependencies relevant to the particular review, including those of advisers. Disclosure is an input to verification, not its result.

**2.2 Examine evidence and practical access.** Use applicable appointment and removal terms, funding arrangements, governance records, conflict disclosures, and direct access checks where lawful and feasible. Check whether records and advice can actually be obtained without permission from the person under review, whether a threatened withdrawal can disable the review, and whether recusal leaves an authorized independent replacement. Record what was checked, by whom, when, the source versions, scope, findings, and missing information. A nominally separate organization, account, or title does not establish independence.

**2.3 Decide eligibility separately from culpability.** Give a bounded, case-specific determination with an expiry or finite reassessment deadline. Unknown required independence is unsatisfied. A material unresolved dependency blocks reliance on that reviewer for the affected independent function; it does not by itself prove misconduct. Use a previously authorized alternative or narrow and defer the dependent action within existing safety limits. Necessary advice requires authorized resources and an adviser whose relevant conflicts are checked; no fee or access power is created here.

**2.4 Reassess on change.** Record changes in funding, appointment, removal, material control, information access, infrastructure, adviser dependence, or case-specific conflicts. A relevant material change makes the affected prior determination insufficient for further reliance until reassessed against the change. Preserve the earlier determination and link the new review to the triggering event. Reassessment must be substantive, not a date refresh. Use published deadlines and interim arrangements without automatic extension of a restriction.

**2.5 Make the verification challengeable.** Affected parties may challenge the owner, evidence, determination, recusal, or reassessment through an independent route that does not depend solely on the challenged actor. A conflict involving the verification owner uses an already authorized replacement or escalation arrangement. Claims of independence remain open to new evidence even after a checklist passes.

## 3 Consult before consequential policy changes

**3.1 Trigger and scope.** Before adopting a material change to restriction grounds, evidence thresholds, review access, independence arrangements, affected participation, data use, or remedy duties, the existing policy decision function opens a proportionate consultation. Publish the scope and why the change is consequential. A documented minor correction that changes none of these effects may use a shorter procedure under prepublished criteria.

**3.2 Publish a usable proposal.** State the problem and supporting evidence; the unchanged-policy baseline; reasonable alternatives, including less intrusive and non-rule options; the proposed text; likely benefits, burdens and their distribution; uncertainty; implementation resources; and evaluation criteria. Explain the authority and adoption process, comment route, accessible formats, finite response period, and handling of private submissions. Make the criteria available before choosing the preferred result.

**3.3 Reach affected outsiders.** Invite input from affected members and nonmembers, dependent users, people likely to bear burdens, and relevant independent expertise. Record outreach, access barriers, participation gaps, and material interests. Seek perspectives that ordinary internal consultation would miss. Participation does not confer authority or require endorsement, and the most numerous or best-funded submissions do not settle the merits.

**3.4 Answer material input.** Publish a reasoned response connecting material objections and alternatives to retained, revised, or rejected provisions. Related submissions may be grouped if each material issue remains traceable. Explain remaining disagreement, tradeoffs, and uncertainty. Material changes to the proposal or its expected burdens require a further proportionate opportunity to respond before adoption. Silence and lack of submissions do not establish consent.

**3.5 Keep consultation distinct from appeal.** Consultation concerns a proposed policy's design and impacts. It does not decide an individual's complaint, exhaust appeal rights, replace notice and response in a case, or ratify a change. Only the effective old amendment and authorization rules can permit adoption and activation. Any genuinely urgent interim measure must already be authorized, narrowly scoped, time-limited, and reviewable; urgency cannot create a general consultation exemption or indefinite rule.

## 4 Assess outcomes independently before claiming improvement

**4.1 Define the claim and assessor.** Assign independent outcome assessment within the existing audit and correction function, with necessary expertise, verified independence, authorized resources, and access to the evidence. Assess whether the operating process improves warranted decisions and correction in stated conditions, including harms caused by the process itself. More records, passing fixtures, fewer appeals, or more restrictions are not sufficient outcome measures.

**4.2 Begin in shadow mode.** Initial comparative assessment is offline or observational shadow work with no changes to real restrictions, grants, access, case dispositions, or remedies. Experimental recommendations remain proposals. Use only legitimately available data under suitable privacy and research protections. Do not secretly randomize real people's protections or impose burdens for a test. Any later live study requires a separate protocol and the applicable approvals; OPS 1 does not authorize it.

**4.3 Preregister a nonempty study.** Before observing assessment outputs, fix a versioned plan identifying the research question, scope, decision tasks, eligible cases, acquisition or sampling method, evidence sources, independent assessors, comparator procedures, outcome definitions, analysis, uncertainty treatment, stopping rules, and reporting date. State the sample or bounded data-collection target and its justification. Distinguish planned, underway, completed, inconclusive, and stopped work. An empty case list, a schema-valid plan, or a plan without observations is not a completed study or evidence of benefit.

**4.4 Compare the minimal and full procedures fairly.** Specify a minimally sufficient procedure and the full proposed process before comparison. The minimal procedure retains required protections; it is not a deliberately unsafe straw comparator. Apply both to the same eligible case population, evidence availability, task definitions, applicable rules, and predeclared resource conditions. Pair cases or justify allocation, order, and any blinding; disclose contamination and changes. Record actual effort, staffing, latency, and unavailable capabilities for both arms. If conditions differ materially, limit the inference or use a separately identified comparison rather than attribute the difference to the protocol alone.

**4.5 Measure benefits and burdens separately.** At minimum, prespecify mistaken restrictions, missed qualifying harms, decision and review delay, residual injury, and remedy completion. Define the unit, denominator, observation window, source, and ascertainment method for each. Include burden distribution and resource costs. An independent, reasoned reference assessment identifies warranted outcomes and unresolved disagreements; it is not assumed infallible. Separate process failures, factual errors, risk predictions, and normative disagreements. Do not collapse them into one success score.

**4.6 Match claims to observable evidence.** A shadow recommendation can be classified as a mistaken restriction recommendation or a missed warranted protective response under the reference assessment; it cannot show that an actual person was restricted or that a predicted harm occurred. Use legitimately observed outcomes where available, and distinguish them from simulation, expert judgment, proxies, estimates, and missing observations. Report uncertainty, disagreement, missingness, follow-up censoring, case dependence, small-sample limits, and limits of generalization. Audit whether the metric itself detects the intended harm.

**4.7 Protect independent validation.** Existing frozen fixtures remain regression and demonstration material. Do not relabel them as new independent observations. Record exposed development cases, holdout provenance, prior access, and deviations. Where candidate rules or parameters are chosen using outcome data, label those findings exploratory and use an untouched, independently specified assessment for stronger claims. Disclose comparisons tried and selection effects; do not keep changing outcomes or stopping rules to obtain success.

**4.8 Stop and report honestly.** Preregister thresholds or decision rules for unacceptable harm or burden, invalid data, compromised independence, insufficient information, and completion of the collection window. Record who may stop the assessment within existing authority and what happens to records and unresolved cases. Report unfavorable and inconclusive results, protocol deviations, uncertainty, and unavailable outcomes. A recommendation to retain, simplify, revise, or reject the procedure requires reasoned interpretation and any necessary consultation. No study result activates policy or establishes general institutional legitimacy.

## 5 Give reasons that can be compared and corrected

**5.1 Maintain comparable reasons.** The existing decision and audit functions keep a privacy-protected record of material facts, applicable rule version, threshold, reasons, disposition, cumulative burden, and subsequent correction. Search for relevant comparable cases by those features, not protected identity, institutional loyalty, or popularity. Record the scope and limits of the search so that selective citation is visible.

**5.2 Explain similarity and departure.** Identify the cases relied upon, relevant similarities, and material differences. Like cases warrant consistent treatment under the same applicable rule. A departure requires reasons grounded in a relevant difference, a justified policy change, or correction of an earlier error. A generic assertion that every case is unique is insufficient. Past practice cannot create missing authority or override a protected limit.

**5.3 Protect privacy while preserving challenge.** Use minimization, role-based access, pseudonymous identifiers, and appropriate redaction for comparison records. Provide enough reasoning for affected parties to challenge inconsistent treatment without exposing unrelated people's private information. Record material redaction limits and provide authorized independent access where needed. Transparency does not authorize publishing case files or extending retention indefinitely.

**5.4 Reconsider bad precedent.** Preserve an erroneous earlier decision as history, mark it corrected or superseded, and identify the reason and scope of reconsideration. Examine similarly affected cases through existing authority and challenge routes where the error may matter. Explain legitimate reliance and prospective transition arrangements. Consistency is not a duty to repeat an error, and correction cannot create a retrospective offense.

## 6 Follow remedies through to their effects

**6.1 Specify a remedy that can be checked.** For each upheld complaint or accepted correction, the existing decision and correction functions identify the injury, exact complaint and decision versions, remedy scope, existing authority, accountable owner, deadline, necessary funding and other resources, evidence of those resources, affected party's requested relief, completion criteria, independent audit, and escalation route. Distinguish a binding direction within an existing mandate from a recommendation or voluntary undertaking. An allocation reference does not prove that funding is available.

**6.2 Preserve distinct stages.** Record each of the following separately, with dates, actors, version links, evidence, and unknown or incomplete status where appropriate:

- **Ordered or recommended:** an authorized decision specifies the remedy and its legal or institutional limits.
- **Accepted:** the responsible actor acknowledges or commits to the specified work. Refusal does not cancel a valid duty; acceptance does not establish authority, funding, execution, or success.
- **Attempted:** an identifiable action was undertaken against the authorized target and scope, with failures or partial effects preserved.
- **Observed:** evidence records the actual resulting state and its limits. A claim of success is not observation; an unrelated improvement is not automatically an effect of the remedy.
- **Audited:** an appropriately independent reviewer checks the specified observed effects, complaint versions, completion criteria, and residual injury, recording qualifications and disagreement.
- **Reported effective:** an attributed conclusion states which remedial objective the observed and audited evidence supports, over what period, and what remains unresolved. This conclusion is not supplied by the actor's acceptance or by a successful command alone.

These are distinct facts and dependencies, not an assertion that every remedy follows an identical administrative sequence. A compulsory duty does not wait for voluntary acceptance. No later claim may fabricate a missing attempted effect, observation, or required independent audit. A structural relation check verifies supplied references only.

**6.3 Include the affected person's account.** Obtain a practical opportunity for the affected person to describe whether access, records, reputation, costs, delay, or other injury were actually repaired. Preserve disagreement with the operator or auditor and reasons for the final assessment. Do not condition independent verification on their assent, require them to contact the person complained of, or treat silence, withdrawal, inability to respond, or unavailable follow-up as proof of repair.

**6.4 Track residual injury and incomplete repair.** Describe remaining harm, recurrence risk, missing evidence, partial completion, and limits of available authority. Unresolved or unknown material injury prevents an unqualified claim of complete repair. Partial effectiveness may be reported with exact scope and limitations. If full repair is impossible, state what was achieved, what cannot be restored, available separately authorized alternatives, and continuing challenge rights; administrative closure must not be presented as removal of the injury.

**6.5 Follow deadlines and escalate.** The accountable owner checks progress against the fixed deadline and completion criteria, records delays and unmet dependencies, and invokes the already authorized escalation or replacement route when required. Refusal, exhausted funding, failed execution, missing evidence, and unavailable independent audit remain distinguishable blockers. Neither escalation nor a funding request supplies new powers, money, or indefinite permission to keep a restriction in force.

**6.6 Correct the wider record.** Where an error reached others, propose correction to the relevant audience within existing authority, privacy rules, and communication authorization. Consider proportionate prevention of recurrence, including methods, assignments, or policy changes. Close only the exact complaint versions and objectives that the observed effects and required audit resolve. Preserve later appeals, new evidence, and unrelated injuries. Reopen affected conclusions when material new evidence warrants it without erasing the earlier history.

## 7 Adoption and implementation boundary

Adoption must use the effective old amendment process and all applicable principal, registrar, notice, and challenge requirements. Any conflict with an existing protected commitment must stop at the existing contract's limit rather than be resolved by this proposal's own language. Assigning an owner does not appoint anyone or spend funds.

Before operational use, separately approve concrete deadlines, scopes, thresholds, retention and access arrangements, qualified role assignments, finite resources, independent challenge routes, evaluation procedures, and effect-boundary guarantees. Missing required safeguards block the dependent capability. The initial software extension remains opt-in, offline, supplied-record-only, and non-executing. External execution, actual studies, consequential case decisions, and assertions of institutional effectiveness require separate evidence and authorization.

## Design sources and limits

The following primary pages were checked on 9 October 2026. They are design references from their own institutional settings, not law binding CORTAC, adoption instruments, or proof that this protocol works. The detailed clauses above are proposed CORTAC choices.

- The [European Ombudsman's Code, articles 9, 10, 16, and 18](https://www.ombudsman.europa.eu/en/publication/en/3510) addresses relevant factors, consistent decisions and reasoned departures, an opportunity to be heard, and individualized grounds. It informs sections 1 and 5; this proposal does not import the Code's jurisdiction or deadlines.
- The [G20/OECD corporate governance principles, V.E and V.F](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/g20-oecd-principles-of-corporate-governance-2023_ed750b30-en/full-report/component-8.html) discuss independent judgment, material relationships, information access, appropriate resources, and external advice. Section 2 adapts those concerns to already existing CORTAC review functions without creating a corporate board or importing corporate law.
- The [OECD Regulatory Impact Assessment principles, chapter 2](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/regulatory-impact-assessment_7a9638cb-en/full-report/component-5.html) connect problem definition, realistic alternatives, effects, stakeholder input, and monitoring. They inform section 3's consultation design; its exact triggers and response duties are proposed here.
- [NIST AI RMF Core, MEASURE 1.1–1.3, 2.1–2.3 and 2.13](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/) discusses suitable metrics, documented tests, independent assessment contributions, relevant conditions, and assessment of measurement effectiveness. Section 4's specific comparison, preregistration, and stopping requirements are CORTAC proposals, not a claim of NIST certification.
- The [Parliamentary and Health Service Ombudsman's Putting things right](https://www.ombudsman.org.uk/handling-complaint/information-organisations-we-investigate/putting-things-right) describes remedy recommendations, follow-up, deadlines, and escalation, while explicitly distinguishing recommendations from legal enforcement powers. It informs section 6's follow-through discipline without conferring enforcement power on CORTAC.
