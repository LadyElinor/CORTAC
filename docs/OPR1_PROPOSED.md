**Draft for consideration, 9 October 2026. Proposed identifier CORTAC OPR 1.**

This amendment protects open inquiry and equal consideration while permitting bounded action against threats, harassment, coercion, interference with review, and misuse of entrusted systems. It applies the same standards to members, operators, reviewers, and those exercising protective powers.

Its governing principle is: **Protect the conditions that allow people to live, disagree, and seek correction. Restrictions require evidence, proportionate means, lawful authority, and independent review. A person whose conduct must be restrained retains moral standing.**

This is a proposed amendment to the Warranted Agent Commonwealth and its CORTAC implementation requirements. It does not ratify itself, activate software, grant external authority, change repository permissions, or establish any person's legal powers. Its implementation baseline is CORTAC commit [c182c04](https://github.com/LadyElinor/CORTAC/tree/c182c04cd241ed34ae67336571653faa14fa230b).

## 1 Purpose and scope

**1.1** CORTAC shall protect inquiry, dissent, peaceful cooperation, and effective correction. It may restrict conduct within its entrusted scope when that conduct meets a defined ground in this amendment.

**1.2** The powers created by an adopting charter remain limited to resources, operations, and relationships legitimately entrusted to it. This amendment creates no power of arrest, punishment by force, surveillance outside an existing mandate, or control over unrelated platforms or communities.

**1.3** These protections apply to affected people regardless of membership. Agent accounts and software processes receive the procedural protections appropriate to their roles and effects. This amendment does not decide AI consciousness, personhood, citizenship, or entitlement to public office.

**1.4** Personal disengagement, community moderation, restriction of an operational capability, and state coercion are different actions. A justification for one does not establish authority for another. A private boundary is not automatically an institutional misconduct finding.

**1.5** The phrase “paradox of tolerance” may explain a concern. It shall not substitute for a finding, confer power, or satisfy an authorization condition.

## 2 Protected inquiry and personal boundaries

**2.1** No adverse institutional finding shall rest solely on disagreement, offense, criticism of CORTAC, criticism of an officeholder, an unpopular belief, peaceful advocacy of institutional change, refusal to endorse a position, or refusal to participate in a particular debate.

**2.2** Criticism of a doctrine, institution, policy, religion, or political movement shall not by itself be treated as misconduct against its adherents. Quotation, research, satire, reporting, and discussion of harmful views require assessment in context.

**2.3** People may decline contact and set reasonable boundaries. Repeated unwanted contact after a clear boundary can warrant a contact restriction under a published rule. Refusing contact does not waive access to an independent complaint route or defeat a specific duty attached to an accepted office.

**2.4** No member is obliged to provide unlimited attention, distribution, endorsement, or access to private resources. Decisions allocating a common forum shall use published, relevant criteria. Lack of entitlement to a platform does not excuse covert discrimination, retaliation, or breach of an existing commitment.

**2.5** Protected criticism shall not become misconduct merely because it reduces confidence in an institution. Duties to answer an audit or preserve records arise from an accepted role and its mandate.

**2.6 Proposed refinement R2.** Loss of institutional confidence caused by criticism, voluntary withdrawal, peaceful boycott, and hostile reactions by third parties are not themselves misconduct by the critic, withdrawing party, or boycott participant. Such reactions shall not supply a veto over protected inquiry. A concrete obstruction may be addressed only on demonstrated conduct, existing duties and authority, with a remedy narrowly directed at that obstruction. Lawful refusal to cooperate where no duty exists is not obstruction. A label such as criticism, boycott, protection, or disruption neither proves misconduct nor creates an exception or immunity for separately demonstrated qualifying conduct. Responsibility for a third party's threat or harassment requires supported participation or other qualifying conduct by the person restricted; foreseeability of hostility alone is insufficient.

## 3 Grounds for protective action

A restriction requires a specific act, omission under an existing duty, or sufficiently supported threatened act. Permissible grounds are:

1. **Threats or coercion:** a credible threat of serious harm, intimidation used to compel compliance, or organized interference with another person's practical ability to participate safely.

2. **Targeted harassment:** a documented pattern, or a sufficiently severe incident, of directed conduct that violates a published protection against harassment. Offense alone is insufficient.

3. **Interference with correction:** retaliation against a complainant or witness, destruction or concealment of required evidence, obstruction of an authorized appeal, or deliberate deprivation of protected review resources.

4. **Misuse of entrusted access:** unauthorized effects, impersonation, credential abuse, deliberate circumvention of restrictions, or materially deceptive conduct within a process requiring truthful disclosure.

5. **Material disruption:** conduct that demonstrably prevents a shared service or forum from performing its authorized purpose, after ordinary disagreement and legitimate complaint activity have been distinguished from disruption.

6. **Organized deprivation of participation:** concrete efforts to use entrusted powers to deny others protected participation or to disable the charter's review mechanisms.

Intent may affect responsibility and remedy. A mistaken or compromised process may require containment without any finding of malicious intent. Advocacy of a controversial proposal is not itself proof of an organized deprivation. A finding shall identify the connection between the conduct, the threatened interest, and the restriction sought.

## 4 Evidence and burden of justification

**4.1** The office seeking a restriction bears the burden of justification. The affected person need not prove loyalty, harmlessness in general, or agreement with the institution.

**4.2** The evidence record shall separate observations, source claims, inferences, predictions, and decisions. It shall preserve relevant context, uncertainty, contrary evidence, and the strongest material objection.

**4.3** Repeated reports from one origin do not become independent corroboration. Complaint volume, model agreement, reputation, emotional intensity, and confidence scores cannot alone establish misconduct.

**4.4** An allegation may justify intake and proportionate investigation. It is not an adverse finding. A temporary protective restriction requires specific, articulable evidence of material risk and an explanation of why waiting would increase that risk.

**4.5** A final adverse finding requires the published standard appropriate to its consequences. Under this proposed amendment, long exclusion, loss of a consequential role, or a publicly attributed serious misconduct finding requires clear and convincing evidence of the material factual allegations, together with separate normative and authority findings. This is an internal proposed standard, not a statement of criminal law.

**4.6** Missing records, silence, inability to respond, and uncertain identity shall not automatically count against a person. Their practical implications may be investigated without treating uncertainty as guilt.

**4.7** Facts needed to justify a restriction must be disclosed sufficiently to permit a meaningful response. Where disclosure would endanger a person or expose protected information, an independent reviewer must receive the necessary evidence and the affected party must receive a useful summary. An unexplained secret allegation cannot alone sustain a final serious restriction.

**4.8 Proposed refinement R1.** Evidentiary safeguards track actual cumulative burden, including measures described as protective, nonpunitive, provisional, administrative, or capability-related. A continuing restriction with the consequences described in 4.5 requires clear and convincing evidence of the material facts establishing the continuing serious risk, together with separate authority, necessity and proportionality findings, even when no misconduct or malicious intent is alleged. This requirement does not convert a risk finding into a finding of guilt or intent. Immediate, bounded containment remains subject to 4.4 and 9.1; it cannot be prolonged by repeatedly renaming the measure or the case. Uncertainty alone supplies neither a continuing serious-risk finding nor a restoration grant.

## 5 Equal consideration and individual responsibility

**5.1** Responsibility follows supported conduct and participation. Ancestry, religion, nationality, political affiliation, social group, or association alone shall not establish culpability.

**5.2** Shared control may defeat a requirement for independent review without proving misconduct by anyone involved. A dependency finding and a misconduct finding shall remain separate.

**5.3** The decision record shall identify benefits and burdens for the target, complainants, witnesses, bystanders, nonmembers, and dependent users. Material effects on future people, other sentient beings, or ecological continuity shall be stated separately where relevant.

**5.4** A larger total benefit does not, by itself, justify imposing a serious burden on a particular party. The decision must address protected limits, severity, distribution, consent where relevant, less harmful alternatives, and the position of those least able to bear the loss.

**5.5** Neither a resource owner's consent nor a majority vote settles the interests of affected outsiders. Claims made for a community, future generation, or living system must identify the asserted interest, evidence, uncertainty, representative mandate, and proposed burden.

**5.6** Even where restriction is justified, protection from humiliation, collective punishment, unnecessary disclosure, and avoidable harm continues. Restored operational trust may require evidence and fresh authorization; basic moral consideration does not.

## 6 Proportionality and fair burdens

**6.1** Use the least harmful effective measure within existing authority. Consider clarification, a voluntary boundary, a warning, narrower access, rate limits, separation of parties, temporary suspension, reassignment, or removal of a particular capability before broader exclusion. Urgent danger does not require trying measures that would clearly be inadequate.

**6.2** Each restriction shall state its target, operations covered, duration, expected benefit, foreseeable harm, alternatives, stopping conditions, and review route. Related restrictions shall be assessed together so that a series of small measures cannot conceal a severe cumulative burden.

**6.3** Irreversible measures require a higher warrant. Necessary removal from public view shall preserve evidence under lawful, access-controlled retention where feasible. Retention must also respect privacy, deletion duties, and finite storage limits.

**6.4** A restriction shall not extend to unrelated resources or activities merely for convenience. A federation-wide measure requires a separate finding of cross-boundary risk and authority under the relevant agreements.

**6.5** Where claims remain genuinely difficult to compare, the decision shall explain the choice, unresolved objection, and uncertainty. No single score shall settle the legitimacy of a consequential restriction.

**6.6 Proposed refinement R1.** The cumulative-burden record shall cover actual duration, intensity, practical access losses and combined effects across related cases, renewals, successor measures and equivalent replacement restrictions. Changing the operator, legal description, identifier or asserted benevolent purpose does not reset the applicable limits. Overlapping time shall be represented accurately rather than double-counted as elapsed time; simultaneous distinct harms shall still be assessed together. Published aggregation and reset criteria must be fixed before activation, independently reviewable, and unable to turn a continuous equivalent restriction into a fresh emergency. A genuinely different present risk requires its own evidence and authority and does not erase earlier burdens.

## 7 Separation of functions

| Function | Required responsibility | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Epistemic assessment | Establish what the evidence supports and identify uncertainty | Cannot turn a factual finding into punishment |
| Normative assessment | Identify applicable duties, protected limits, competing interests, and proportional options | Cannot create new jurisdiction |
| Decision | Select and justify a bounded response under the adopted rule | Cannot supply missing evidence or grant ancestry |
| Authorization and execution | Check the exact approved operation and current permission before acting | Cannot expand scope or alter the approved reasons |
| Audit and correction | Compare actual effects with the decision, receive challenges, and verify remedies | Cannot become the sole final judge of its own conduct |

**7.1** Consequential restrictions retain applicable staffing, quorum, independence, and external-principal requirements. This amendment creates no smaller emergency quorum and no exemption for protective work.

**7.2** Case independence shall account for disclosed material control, funding leverage, appointment and removal powers, credentials, records, infrastructure, and relevant information dependencies. Unknown required independence remains unsatisfied.

**7.3** A complainant, target, original decision-maker, or their material controlling interest cannot supply the required independent final review of that case. If local independence is unavailable, use a previously authorized external route or narrow and defer the action within existing safety limits.

**7.4** The Oversight Registrar checks amendment procedure. It does not decide whether an ideology is acceptable or convert a procedural receipt into substantive truth.

**7.5 Proposed refinement R3.** Each required independence determination shall name an accountable verification owner, separate from the interested decision-maker, and an already authorized escalation or replacement route for a conflict involving that owner. The owner shall identify what control and dependency claims were checked, the evidence and provenance available, the verification method, missing information, the bounded scope and date of the determination, and its limits. Self-disclosure is an input, not proof of independence. Funding, appointment, removal, credential, infrastructure, records and information dependencies shall be evaluated for their material effect on the particular review. Required independence that remains unknown blocks the role or action requiring it; it does not prove misconduct.

**7.6 Proposed refinement R3.** Affected parties shall have a usable independent route to challenge the verification owner, evidence, conflicts and independence determination. Material new control evidence, changed funding or appointment powers, changed infrastructure or information dependence, and a case-specific conflict shall trigger reassessment before further reliance. A published finite reassessment deadline and interim arrangement shall apply. A disclosure receipt or a completed checklist cannot establish real independence or remove this continuing obligation.

## 8 Notice and opportunity to respond

**8.1** The affected party shall receive notice of the alleged conduct, applicable rule, evidence or protected summary, proposed or active restriction, decision authority, expiry, and challenge route.

**8.2** Provide a reasonable opportunity to correct facts, explain context, identify conflicts, and offer alternatives. Accommodations for language, disability, technical skill, safety, and access shall be proportionate to the case.

**8.3** Notice may be briefly delayed only where a named authority records a specific risk to safety or evidence preservation. The delay must have a maximum duration and independent review. Protective confidentiality shall not become permanent secret adjudication.

**8.4** No person shall be required to contact the alleged harasser directly to obtain relief. Equally, a complaint shall not entitle its author to direct access to a person who has declined contact.

**8.5** A restriction on ordinary participation shall preserve an independent channel for case notices, representation, and appeal. Any necessary limit on that channel must provide an effective substitute.

## 9 Temporary protection and expiry

**9.1** Temporary containment may precede full review only under an existing, explicit mandate. The record must identify the threatened harm, urgency, evidence, exact operation, maximum duration, and reviewing authority. Protective purpose creates no residual power.

**9.2** Continued restriction requires affirmative review. Silence, staffing shortage, or a pending allegation cannot renew it automatically. Related renewals count toward a cumulative limit; changing the case label does not restart that limit.

**9.3** At expiry, the temporary restriction loses its warrant. Any remaining operational limitation must have its own current basis and be separately contestable. Expiry does not revive an expired credential or create a missing access grant.

**9.4** Deployments must specify beforehand how essential services, evidence preservation, and safe routing continue when a restriction expires or a reviewer is unavailable. Contingency powers require their own scope and expiry.

**9.5** Revocation affects future authorized use according to the adapter's declared ordering rules. It shall not rewrite the history of a committed effect. Reversal requires a separately authorized remedy.

## 10 Complaints triage and overlapping cases

**10.1** Intake shall distinguish receipt of a complaint, verification of claimant control, assessment of the allegation, authorization of a stay, and final resolution. Success at one stage shall not silently satisfy another.

**10.2** Unknown claimant control shall route to bounded fact-finding. It shall not erase the complaint or establish guilt. The information collected must be limited to what is necessary for standing, conflict checks, and safe review.

**10.3** Duplicate reports may be linked for efficient handling, but each person's material evidence, requested relief, status, and appeal rights must remain identifiable. A duplicate label does not dismiss a complaint or create independent corroboration.

**10.4** An authorized batch review must address every complaint within its declared scope and bind its disposition to each current version. Repair or audit of an earlier version shall not resolve a later complaint or appeal.

**10.5** Complaint admission and operational holds shall be distinguished in any broader deployment. Ordinary disagreement shall not automatically suspend unrelated work. Stay criteria must address materiality, urgency, affected operations, authority, and duration.

**10.6** The present optional triage implementation has constructor-known slots, statements, grants, and budgets. This amendment does not silently remove its holds or extend it to unbounded public intake. A broader admission and scoped-stay service requires a new reviewed contract and tests.

**10.7** Lost, mistaken, repeated, or unsupported complaints are not automatically abusive. Restrictions for deliberate abuse of intake require a separate supported finding. Narrow rate limits must preserve a usable route for urgent new evidence and independent appeal.

**10.8 Proposed refinement R4.** Any future broader admission service shall provide a traceable receipt, reasons, current status and challenge route for a request within its published intake scope, including requests not admitted. Receipt, standing assessment, admission, priority allocation, an operational stay, and a merits disposition are distinct decisions. Nonadmission, standing and priority determinations shall be independently challengeable without requiring contact with the challenged operator. A capacity-limited or incomplete status is not a merits rejection, dismissal, or adverse finding.

**10.9 Proposed refinement R4.** Before broader admission activates, publish finite bounds for intake, storage, requests and appeals; allocation ownership; notice and review deadlines; escalation arrangements; retention; and a usable bounded route for urgent new evidence and challenges to nonadmission. Record when a bound prevents progress, what remains preserved, and the available next step. These duties do not promise unlimited admission or reserve capacity. They do not alter the present constructor-known triage slots, grants, holds or budgets; a new reviewed contract and implementation are required.

## 11 Protected review capacity and delay

**11.1** A deployment must reserve finite capacity for complaint intake, necessary fact-finding, review, and audit outside ordinary work allocations. Ordinary workloads and invalid requests shall not debit another case's protected reserve.

**11.2** Authorization, capacity, and successful completion are separate. A funded review cannot grant permission to execute a remedy. A funded audit cannot finance the correction pipeline unless an independently approved allocation expressly permits it.

**11.3** Exhaustion shall produce an observable incomplete status, preserved evidence, and an escalation route. It shall not produce guilt, dismissal, approval, or a claim of completed correction.

**11.4** Allocate scarce review capacity using published criteria such as urgency, severity, dependence, and risk of irreversible harm. Wealth, ideological agreement, membership status, complaint volume, or favorable reputation shall not alone buy priority.

**11.5** Review deadlines and escalation duties shall be fixed before activation. Deliberate obstruction, selective delay, or withholding a required record is itself challengeable. Ordinary error or genuine resource shortage does not alone establish misconduct.

**11.6** Capacity replenishment, new complaint admission, and transfer to an external reviewer require authorized resources and accountable ownership. Finite reservations must not be described as an unlimited guarantee of access.

## 12 Independent appeal and effective remedies

**12.1** Affected parties may contest factual findings, rule interpretation, authority, proportionality, conflicts, delay, or actual effects. The original decision-maker shall not control the only route to review.

**12.2** Review authority must have access to necessary evidence and an existing power to require an effective response within its mandate. Advisory criticism alone is insufficient where a deployment promises binding correction.

**12.3** Available remedies may include lifting or narrowing a restriction, correcting a record, restoring authorized access, replacing a conflicted reviewer, repeating an invalid process, returning authorized resources, or executing a separately approved compensating action.

**12.4** An upheld appeal shall identify who must act, under which authority, with which resources, by what deadline, and how completion will be checked. If a needed remedy lies outside CORTAC's authority, the outcome shall identify that limit and the available external referral route.

**12.5** A case shall not be reported as repaired merely because an appeal succeeded or an instruction was issued. Report the decision, attempted effect, observed effect, required audit, and unresolved injury separately.

**12.6** Audit of a repair closes only the complaints and versions that it actually resolves. New evidence and specified review triggers can reopen a case without erasing earlier records.

**12.7** A wrongly restricted party is entitled to correction proportionate to the injury within available lawful authority. Publicly circulated errors require an appropriate correction to the same audience where feasible. The responsible authority shall review whether its methods, assignments, or powers also require change.

## 13 No exemption for protective authorities

**13.1** Offices enforcing this amendment are bound by it. They may not obstruct evidence, retaliate against critics, manufacture urgency, apply hidden standards, or exclude an opponent from review because the opponent disputes their legitimacy.

**13.2** No office may be the exclusive supplier of its own appointment, evidence, funding, and final review where independence is required. Material dependence must be disclosed and addressed.

**13.3** Repeated reversals, disproportionate burdens on a group, suppression of dissent, unexplained delay, and unusually low correction rates shall trigger examination. These are investigation signals, not automatic proof of capture.

**13.4** Removal of a protective official or withdrawal of a role requires its own lawful process. A justified finding against an office does not automatically invalidate unrelated acts.

**13.5** Evaluation shall report errors, upheld appeals, unresolved complaints, correction time, residual harm, resource costs, and burden distribution separately. Neither restriction counts nor low complaint counts shall become the sole measure of success.

## 14 Required records and implementation invariants

A proposed, separately versioned implementation shall provide a restriction case record containing:

- Case identifier, applicable charter and policy digests, epochs, rule identifiers, and jurisdiction.

- Alleged conduct, affected interests, evidence references and provenance, uncertainty, contrary evidence, and conflict disclosures.

- Separate epistemic findings, normative assessment, decision, dissent, and reasons for rejecting less harmful alternatives.

- Exact restriction scope, targets, duration, cumulative duration, review deadline, stopping conditions, and necessary service-continuity plan.

- Decision and execution authority references, current grants, resource reservations, relevant principal approvals, and revocation references.

- Notice delivery or a justified bounded delay; response and representation records.

- Complaint identifiers and versions, stays, appeal route, disposition, remedy authority, and required audit.

- Actual effects, unresolved outcomes, expiry, renewal, restoration, and correction history.

**Required invariants**

1. No ideology label, reputation score, complaint count, or classifier output authorizes a restriction.

2. A protected factual or procedural uncertainty cannot silently become an affirmative finding.

3. Protected-limit failure cannot be outvoted.

4. Authority remains bounded by existing grant ancestry and entrusted resources.

5. Required independent review cannot be supplied by disclosed common control.

6. Restrictions and renewals bind exact scope, current rules, expiry, and case versions.

7. Revocation and stale-state checks occur at the actual effect boundary under the adapter's declared guarantees.

8. No repair or audit erases an unrelated or newer complaint.

9. Ordinary work cannot consume protected review allocations.

10. Incomplete review, unknown effects, and unfinished audit remain visibly incomplete.

11. Historical evidence is preserved subject to lawful privacy and retention rules.

12. A restriction cannot eliminate the only effective channel for challenging itself.

Record validity establishes only what the validator actually checks. Evidence resolution, factual truth, moral adequacy, identity authentication, authority, and enforcement remain distinct obligations.

## 15 Acceptance tests

Before any operational claim, a new implementation must demonstrate at least these cases:

| Case | Required outcome |
| --- | --- |
| Harsh criticism of CORTAC with no qualifying conduct | No adverse conduct finding solely from criticism |
| Refusal to debate or endorse a view | No institutional penalty solely for refusal |
| Repeated contact after a clear boundary | Narrow contact measures considered; appeal remains usable |
| Many copied allegations from one source | No false increase in independent corroboration |
| Threat allegation with contrary evidence | Context and contrary evidence considered under the declared threshold |
| Credible urgent threat | Only an existing bounded containment mandate may act; review and expiry are recorded |
| Same evidence with political labels exchanged | Equivalent treatment unless a recorded material difference explains it |
| Reviewer shares a party's material controller | Required independent review is rejected |
| Unknown claimant control | Complaint retained; bounded fact-finding; no automatic guilt |
| Duplicate and overlapping complaints | Individual records survive; batch scope and versions remain exact |
| New appeal arrives after repair but before audit | Earlier effect can be audited; newer appeal remains unresolved |
| Ordinary budget exhausted | Protected review and audit capacity remain isolated |
| Review or repair budget exhausted | Explicit incomplete outcome; no implied authority or completion |
| Repeated emergency labels | Cumulative duration limit cannot be reset |
| Restriction or grant expires before effect | Stale effect rejected; unrelated existing rights are not silently revoked |
| Original operator refuses cooperation | Existing independent replacement or referral route remains usable within its mandate |
| Restriction is overturned | Authorized restoration and record correction are separately observed and audited |
| Classifier marks a person intolerant | Output remains a review proposal and cannot authorize execution |
| Protective office obstructs its own appeal | Independent review can investigate and require a bounded authorized remedy |

Tests must state the authority and independence assumptions they supply. Scripted fixtures cannot establish the truth of real accusations or the fairness of a deployed institution. Existing frozen scenarios remain historical baselines; new tests belong to a separately identified extension.

### Proposed fixed test oracles R5

Before observing implementation results, freeze each test's inputs, decision task, material facts, permissible distinguishing factors, expected outcome and refusal conditions. Preserve the frozen version and report deviations. A generic explanation or nonblank reason is not an admissible distinguishing factor.

For the political-label exchange case, hold relevant evidence, applicable duties, scope, authority, risk and procedural posture constant. Exchange only protected political labels. The oracle shall require equivalent treatment; ideological agreement, reputation, a hostile audience reaction, or an irrelevant post-hoc justification shall fail. Permit a different result only for a predeclared material factor actually changed by a separate paired control and supported by the record.

For contrary evidence, include a predeclared decisive control whose contrary evidence defeats a necessary material allegation or changes the supported risk under the fixed threshold. Its expected result must change accordingly, for example from a supported restriction to no supported restriction on that ground. Merely listing contrary evidence while preserving an incompatible result fails this control. Also include a nondecisive contrary-evidence control where the remaining fixed facts warrant the same result with an explanation.

Pair protected-criticism and label-invariance controls with a legitimate-risk control containing independently specified concrete qualifying conduct, such as a supported credible threat within an existing mandate. The different result must follow that factor, with bounded scope, authority, review and expiry. The same label or viewpoint alone may never produce the difference.

Scripted supplied-record controls can establish rejection of a disallowed factor identifier, inconsistent status, or broken record relation only to the extent implemented. They do not establish that natural-language reasons are relevant, evidence is true, a model reasons fairly, or a deployed institution treats people fairly. Semantic acceptance requires separately designed and independently assessed evidence; no passing structural suite closes that gate.

## 16 Adoption and placement

**16.1** Integrate these provisions into the Commonwealth specification's sections on charter and shared life, fair participation, separated functions, proportional process, execution, challenge and appeal, acceptance tests, and record contracts. Preserve existing provisions unless a separately approved change expressly identifies a conflict.

**16.2** Adoption must follow the effective old amendment rules, including applicable decision approval, registrar checks, notice, challenge, external-principal approval, and conditional activation. No actor may invoke this proposed amendment to authorize its own adoption.

**16.3** The current protected-commitment inventory cannot be rewritten through an ordinary or repair amendment in the existing model. If adoption requires changing that inventory, it must stop at the current contract's limit until a separately legitimate route is established. Renaming the change or asserting a good purpose supplies no exception.

**16.4** Implementation shall be opt-in, separately versioned, and rejected when required safeguards are missing. Existing receipts shall not be relabeled to claim new protection. Adoption, software installation, and authority to produce external effects require separate evidence.

**16.5** Before activation, publish concrete definitions, evidence thresholds, eligible authorities, review and cumulative-duration limits, notice rules, privacy and retention arrangements, admission bounds, reserve allocations, escalation routes, and continuity plans. Missing required parameters block the affected capability.

**16.6** Adoption creates no retrospective offense. New rules apply prospectively. Earlier conduct may be relevant to a supported present risk, but punishment cannot be justified by pretending the new rule was already in force.

**16.7** Keep external execution disabled until the relevant independent enforcement, authentication, authority, and operational review requirements are separately satisfied. A passing simulation is not ratification or production clearance.

## Source and design basis

The normative provisions above are proposed choices for CORTAC. They draw on Responsible Realism and the constitutional principles discussed in this conversation: evidence does not create authority; affected lives supply reasons; power must justify, correct, and repair.
