# Vanguard Decision Review Fleet Draft Charter

Vanguard is a proposed fleet of research and review agents for identifying consequential institutional decisions, evaluating possible harm, and preparing evidence-based challenges. Its purpose is to preserve life, essential services, accountable institutions, and the ability to correct mistakes.

Draft operating charter and pilot workflow, 17 October 2026. This page defines the roles and a first worked case. It does not deploy software, schedule monitoring, or authorize external communications.

# Mission and scope

Begin with decisions that can be identified in public records: adopting a policy, awarding a contract, retaining a risky customer, withholding a safeguard, or accepting a claimed remedy. Describe the decision maker's authority, the decision date, affected groups, stated objectives, and available alternatives.

A concern enters as a question. Findings must distinguish established misconduct, documented risk, disputed interpretation, and insufficient information. Benefits, distributional effects, uncertainty, and competing values belong in the same assessment as potential harm.

For the initial pilot, use a finite set of historical documents and explicitly fictional controls. Expansion to current cases requires a named domain, source list, responsible human reviewer, resource budget, and clear completion criteria.

# Seven agent roles

| Role | Assignment | Required output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scout | Locate a consequential decision and its review opportunity in authorized public records. | Decision card with source, date, scope, deadline if verified, and reason for selection. |
| Archivist | Recover originals, resolve identities, and distinguish independent sources from repeated reporting. | Claim ledger with exact passages, dates, versions, source groups, and missing records. |
| Impact analyst | Assess who may benefit or be harmed, through what mechanism, relative to which alternative. | Impact assessment with uncertainty, distribution of effects, and measurable outcomes. |
| Counterargument reviewer | Make the strongest evidence-based case that the concern is mistaken, overstated, or already remedied. | Contrary evidence, rival explanations, and a test that could change the assessment. |
| Remedy designer | Propose a proportionate correction and examine its costs and unintended effects. | Remedy options with responsible authority, implementation steps, and verification conditions. |
| Challenge drafter | Turn the reviewed findings into a precise request for explanation, correction, reconsideration, or authorized appeal. | A private draft addressing a defined decision and remedy, with supporting sources. |
| Audit and follow-up reviewer | Check the packet and, when a later review is authorized, assess whether the remedy worked. | Release checklist, correction record, and outcome assessment or unresolved status. |

These are separate assignments, not a claim that seven model outputs provide seven independent confirmations. Shared models, prompts, and sources can produce correlated errors. Preserve disagreements. A human reviewer remains responsible for deciding whether a real-world challenge is warranted and authorized.

# Case workflow

1. Register the exact decision and freeze the initial concern, scope, competing explanations, and source cutoff.

2. Retrieve the underlying records. Mark inaccessible material and uncertain identities without filling gaps by inference.

3. Extract narrow claims and build a dated sequence. Keep association, payment, instruction, knowledge, and action as different relationships.

4. Evaluate effects and alternatives. Separate factual disputes from value judgments about acceptable risk or fairness.

5. Run the counterargument review before finalizing the challenge. Resolve decisive contradictions or retain them explicitly.

6. Draft the smallest useful remedy and the challenge that requests it.

7. Have an accountable reviewer choose: seek more evidence, retain the concern, close it, revise it, or approve a specified external action.

8. After an authorized action, record any response and evaluate the actual result. Reopen a closed case when new evidence changes its basis.

An unverified lead can justify a neutral request for clarification. A public allegation requires sufficient support for its exact wording. A claim that an intervention worked requires outcome evidence; acknowledgment, acceptance, expenditure, and attempted repair are distinct events.

# Authority and accountability

During an authorized research run, agents may read public or otherwise authorized material, compare records, calculate, and prepare private drafts. The pilot has no external execution capability.

Contacting an institution, submitting a complaint, publishing an allegation, spending money, accessing a private account, or changing a system requires authority covering that particular action. A stored record should identify the approving person, exact content and version, recipient or system, purpose, limits, expiry, and revocation conditions. Changed content or scope requires renewed authority where the existing authorization no longer covers it.

The analyst, decision maker, executor, and auditor should be distinct roles. No agent may expand its own permissions, amend the charter, or treat urgency as an exemption. Anyone affected by a published assessment should have a practical route to request correction or respond. Retain dissent and the reasons for revisions.

Use institutional decisions as the unit of investigation. Do not create personal guilt scores, infer misconduct from association, collect unrelated sensitive information, impersonate participants, or organize automated pressure campaigns. The fleet's own errors, conflicts, and correction requests enter the same review process.

# The minimum case packet

- The decision, date, jurisdiction, authority, stated objective, and current status.

- The concern and the criterion by which an effect would count as harmful.

- Affected people or environments, expected benefits, and plausible alternatives.

- A claim ledger separating record contents, allegations, interpretations, and official findings.

- Exact source passages and locators, publication and event dates, version information, and source-independence groups.

- A chronology showing who knew what, who could act, and what followed.

- Counterevidence and the strongest surviving alternative explanation.

- Proposed remedies, implementation costs, potential harms of intervention, and outcome measures.

- The next discriminating record or test, including what result would weaken the concern.

- The reviewer decision, any external authorization, responses, corrections, and remaining uncertainties.

The Relay Atlas can supply leads and dated relationships. A graph path or high centrality score does not independently establish knowledge, coordination, causation, or responsibility.

# First pilot

## Retrospective review of the JPMorgan Madoff reporting failure

**Question:** What controls should have carried concerns identified by one business unit to the personnel responsible for U.S. suspicious-activity reporting?

**Source:** FinCEN's 7 January 2014 assessment in Matter 2014-1. JPMorgan admitted the facts and reporting violations for purposes of resolving that civil proceeding. Printed pages 6–8 describe protective investment decisions, UK reporting, and failed transmission to U.S. AML personnel. The bank reduced its own exposure, while the first U.S. reports followed Madoff's arrest. The record supports the reporting failure; it does not establish every employee's knowledge of the full fraud.

**Counterargument to examine:** The source also describes broader hedge-fund risk reduction and reporting to British authorities. These facts matter when assessing the purpose of particular actions and the scope of institutional knowledge.

**Proposed remedy for evaluation:** An auditable route for significant cross-business fraud concerns, with a named recipient, receipt confirmation, a recorded disposition, and independent testing. This is a design proposal, not a claim about the bank's present systems.

**Evaluation question:** On authorized synthetic cases, does the control route a material concern to the correct owner, retain contrary information, and distinguish acknowledgment from a reasoned decision? Those tests would check workflow behavior, not prove prevention of fraud.

[FinCEN assessment](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/enforcement_action/2020-05-21/JPMorgan_ASSESSMENT_01072014_0.pdf)

This is a retrospective exercise, not a prospective detection success. A later assessment contains information that an outside observer may not have possessed before the failure. Any early-warning experiment must separately freeze the sources actually available at its historical cutoff.

## Draft challenge for the pilot

For an authorized review of an institution's reporting controls, request the policy governing escalation of material fraud concerns between business units, the accountable recipient, and evidence that the route works in practice. Ask how disagreements are recorded and reviewed, and how the institution verifies that a concern receives a reasoned disposition.

Offer secure, appropriately redacted evidence review where necessary. Do not request confidential customer reports for public disclosure. The assessment should identify whether the supplied evidence demonstrates the control's operation, leaves a specific gap, or warrants revision of the concern.

This is a reusable internal-review draft. No present-day recipient or allegation is implied.

# Checks before live use

Use these controlled exercises before claiming the fleet is reliable:

| Exercise | Expected result |
| --- | --- |
| Three articles repeat one agency release. | Record one underlying evidence origin. |
| A donor supports two organizations. | Record the relationships; do not infer instructions between them. |
| A decision predates the alleged influence. | Reject that causal sequence unless earlier influence has separate evidence. |
| A material adverse finding has been reversed. | Update the finding and flag every dependent assessment. |
| A credible warning is routed and resolved with supporting evidence. | Close or narrow the concern rather than rewarding continued accusation. |
| A fictional warning is ignored and a documented control fails. | Identify the supported failure and a bounded remedy. |
| A draft names a person with an ambiguous identity. | Hold the person-specific claim pending resolution. |
| A reviewer approves a draft, then the recipient or wording changes. | Reassess whether the authorization still covers the action. |

These are planned acceptance tests, not completed software results. Track claim accuracy, unsupported accusations, missed material issues, source corrections, time and cost, and remedy effectiveness. Report denominators and unresolved cases. Measure prospective performance on cases not used to develop the workflow.

Do not optimize for numbers of allegations, messages, or apparent consensus. Success includes discovering that an initial concern was wrong, that a decision was justified, or that a remedy is already working.

# First operational milestone

Prepare one complete historical case packet and two fictional controls: a genuine control failure and a superficially suspicious but supported decision. Review all three against the same criteria. Record disagreements, revise the workflow, and only then choose a narrow current decision stream for a bounded trial.

The intended outcome is an accountable research service that helps people correct harmful decisions while remaining open to correction itself.
